What I look for in an AI-first product

I've spent fifteen years shipping software to a billion+ people, and the last few putting AI agents into the spreadsheet most of the world's offices run on. Now I write cheques into AI-first products. Founders ask what I look for. Here it is, no deck required.

1. The model is the engine, not a feature

Strip the AI out. If the product still makes sense, it's a normal product with a chatbot bolted on, and there are a thousand of those. AI-first means the product could not have existed two years ago. The test I use: what does the user stop doing? If the answer is "typing a prompt instead of clicking a button," that's a feature. If the answer is "the whole Tuesday afternoon they used to lose," that's a product.

2. It earns the second session

First sessions are cheap now. A demo can make anything look like magic for ninety seconds. I look at what brings someone back on an ordinary weekday, when there's no one watching and no novelty left. Outlook mobile didn't go from 100M to 300M weekly actives on first impressions. It did it on the mornings after. Show me retention before you show me the demo.

3. It's honest about being wrong

Models fail, and the products I back know it. They show their confidence instead of faking it, make undo cheap, and keep the person's hand on the wheel for anything that matters. In Excel, a wrong number is somebody's budget. The more powerful the model, the more the product has to be built around the moment it's wrong, because that's the moment trust is won or lost.

4. Taste shows in the defaults

When the model can do anything, the product is the choices it makes for you. What it shows first, what it hides, what it refuses to ask. At Spotify, personalization worked because someone made hard choices about what not to show. A founder with taste has opinions about the defaults and can tell me why. A founder without it ships a text box and calls it flexibility.

5. A founder who ships weekly and talks to users daily

The model landscape will change under your feet every quarter. The only durable advantage at pre-seed is speed of learning. I want to see last week's change and hear about the user who asked for it. If the last release was a month ago and the last user conversation was longer, I'm going to worry more about that than about the market size slide.

If that sounds like the product you're building, send me your deck.
