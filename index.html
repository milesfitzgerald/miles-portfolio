The problem

In expert-judgment domains — legal review, clinical documentation, financial analysis, design critique — teams ship agents they cannot measure.

The usual substitutes don't hold. LLM-as-judge inherits the same blind spots as the system under test. Crowdsourced labels can't reach the judgment you're trying to automate. And a rubric written in a planning doc encodes what someone assumed experts do, which is rarely what they actually do.

What's missing isn't tooling. It's a defensible answer to a simple question: according to whom, and how would you know?

Method

  1. Scope the judgment

    Identify the specific decisions where your agent's output is accepted or rejected, and who does the accepting.

  2. Elicit from practitioners

    Structured think-aloud sessions with working domain experts. They reason through real cases while the criteria they're using get surfaced and recorded.

  3. Code the transcripts

    Reasoning gets turned into a coding scheme, applied independently, and checked for agreement between raters. Disagreement is data — it shows you where quality is genuinely contested.

  4. Build the labelled set

    A validated ground truth set with documented provenance: who judged, on what basis, and how consistently.

  5. Hand over the harness

    Your team runs it. The set is versioned and extendable, so it survives the next model swap.
